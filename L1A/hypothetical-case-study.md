# L-1A Hypothetical Case Study

## 1. Case Overview

### Background

A Pakistani entrepreneur owns and operates a technology consulting company in Pakistan.

The company has been operating for several years and provides web development, software solutions, and digital consulting services to business clients.

The owner intends to establish a U.S. subsidiary and relocate temporarily to the United States to develop and manage the new operation.

The proposed case is analyzed as a hypothetical L-1A new-office scenario.

## 2. Foreign Entity

- Country: Pakistan
- Business: Technology Consulting
- Ownership: 100% owned by the beneficiary
- Operations: Established business with existing clients
- Employees: Existing staff supporting business operations
- Beneficiary's role: Owner and senior manager

## 3. Proposed U.S. Entity

- Structure: U.S. subsidiary
- Business: Technology and digital consulting
- Proposed location: United States
- Initial operations: Business development, client acquisition, project management, and technology consulting
- Proposed staffing: Business development and technical personnel
- Beneficiary's proposed role: Executive/managerial leadership

## 4. Legal Issues to Analyze

The case should be reviewed under the following areas:

### A. Qualifying Relationship

Determine whether the ownership and organizational structure establish a qualifying relationship between the foreign and U.S. entities.

### B. Foreign Employment

Determine whether the beneficiary satisfies the applicable foreign employment requirement and whether the foreign position involved qualifying managerial or executive responsibilities.

### C. Proposed U.S. Position

Analyze whether the proposed U.S. position primarily involves qualifying managerial or executive responsibilities.

### D. New Office Requirements

Analyze:

- Physical premises
- Proposed nature and scope of the U.S. business
- Organizational structure
- Financial goals
- U.S. investment
- Foreign entity's financial ability
- Staffing plan
- Expected development during the first year

## 5. Evidence Framework

Potential evidence may include:

- Pakistani company registration documents
- U.S. incorporation documents
- Ownership records
- Organizational charts
- Employment records
- Payroll documentation
- Tax records
- Business contracts
- Client records
- Financial statements
- Business plan
- Lease or premises documentation
- Investment records
- Staffing plan
- Proposed job description
- Market research
- Financial projections

## 6. Key Research Questions

1. Is the relationship between the foreign and U.S. entities sufficiently documented?
2. Does the beneficiary satisfy the required foreign employment period?
3. What evidence demonstrates the beneficiary's actual foreign duties?
4. Are the proposed U.S. duties primarily managerial or executive?
5. Who will perform operational and technical work?
6. What employees will report to the beneficiary?
7. Does the business plan provide a credible development structure?
8. Can the proposed U.S. operation support the beneficiary's managerial or executive position within the required period?

## 8. Research Method

The case should be analyzed by connecting each legal requirement with specific supporting evidence.

The researcher should avoid relying only on job titles or general statements about the beneficiary's role.

The analysis should examine the beneficiary's actual duties, organizational authority, staffing structure, and proposed development of the U.S. business.

## 9. Preliminary Research Conclusion

This hypothetical case demonstrates the importance of analyzing an L-1A petition through separate legal and evidentiary categories.

Further research should verify each requirement against:

- INA § 101(a)(44)
- 8 CFR § 214.2(l)
- USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 2, Part L
- Relevant USCIS Administrative Appeals Office decisions
