This framework provides a structured method for analyzing an L-1A case based on the facts, legal requirements, organizational structure, and available evidence.

## 3. Qualifying Relationship

### Questions

- What is the relationship between the foreign and U.S. entities?
- Is the ownership structure documented?
- Are corporate records available?
- Is the relationship supported by reliable evidence?

### Evidence

- Articles of incorporation
- Shareholding records
- Ownership documents
- Corporate records
- Organizational charts
- Financial records

## 4. Beneficiary's Foreign Employment

### Questions

- How long has the beneficiary worked for the foreign entity?
- What position does the beneficiary hold?
- What are the beneficiary's actual duties?
- Does the evidence support the claimed managerial or executive role?

### Evidence

- Employment agreement
- Payroll records
- Tax records
- Job description
- Organizational chart
- Employment verification
- Business records

## 5. Proposed U.S. Position

### Questions

- What position will the beneficiary hold?
- What are the proposed duties?
- Who will report to the beneficiary?
- What decision-making authority will the beneficiary have?
- What operational duties will be performed by other employees?

## 6. Managerial or Executive Capacity

### Analysis

The case should be analyzed based on the beneficiary's actual proposed duties, authority, organizational structure, staffing, and business operations.

The analysis should distinguish qualifying managerial or executive responsibilities from routine operational or administrative duties.

## 7. New Office Analysis

If the case involves a new U.S. office, separately examine:

- Physical premises
- Proposed business activities
- Business plan
- Investment
- Financial projections
- Staffing plan
- Hiring timeline
- Organizational structure
- Expected business development
- Ability to support the proposed position

## 9. Risk-Assessment Questions

Consider:

1. Are the corporate relationships clearly documented?
2. Is the beneficiary's foreign employment adequately supported?
3. Are the actual duties consistent with the claimed position?
4. Does the organizational structure support the proposed role?
5. Are operational duties properly allocated?
6. If a new office is involved, is the proposed development of the business adequately documented?
7. Are there inconsistencies between the business plan and supporting evidence?
