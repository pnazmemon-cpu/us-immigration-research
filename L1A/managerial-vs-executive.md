# L-1A Managerial vs. Executive Capacity

## 1. Purpose

This research note compares managerial capacity and executive capacity under the L-1A classification.

The analysis focuses on the beneficiary's actual duties, authority, organizational structure, staffing, and role within the business.

## 2. Managerial Capacity

Managerial capacity generally involves an assignment in which the employee primarily:

- Manages the organization, department, subdivision, function, or component
- Supervises and controls qualifying employees or manages an essential function
- Exercises authority over personnel actions or functions at a senior level
- Exercises discretion over day-to-day operations of the activity or function

## 3. Executive Capacity

Executive capacity generally involves an assignment in which the employee primarily:

- Directs the management of the organization or a major component or function
- Establishes organizational goals and policies
- Exercises wide latitude in discretionary decision-making
- Receives only general supervision or direction from higher-level executives, the board of directors, or stockholders

## 4. Key Evidence Factors

A professional L-1A analysis should consider:

- Actual job duties
- Organizational structure
- Staffing levels
- Position within the organization
- Scope of authority
- Personnel responsibilities
- Decision-making authority
- Nature and scope of the business
- Work performed by other employees
- Operational duties performed by the beneficiary

USCIS evaluates the totality of the evidence rather than relying only on a job title or the number of employees supervised.

## 5. Managerial vs. Executive Comparison

| Issue | Managerial Capacity | Executive Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Primary focus | Management of organization, department, function, or component | Direction of management and major organizational functions |
| Personnel authority | May involve supervision and control of qualifying employees | May involve directing managers or other organizational leadership |
| Decision-making | Discretion over relevant day-to-day operations | Wide latitude in organizational decision-making |
| Organizational role | Management of an organization, function, or component | Direction of management and establishment of goals and policies |
| Evidence | Organizational chart, duties, staffing, authority, personnel records | Organizational chart, strategic authority, goals, policies, decision-making evidence |

## 6. Operational Duties

The analysis should distinguish qualifying managerial or executive duties from operational or administrative duties.

A beneficiary may be involved in the early stages of a business, particularly in a new-office case. However, the evidence should demonstrate how the beneficiary's role develops into a position primarily involving qualifying managerial or executive responsibilities.

## 7. Research Questions

When reviewing an L-1A case, consider:

1. What is the beneficiary's actual position?
2. What are the beneficiary's primary duties?
3. Who reports to the beneficiary?
4. What personnel authority does the beneficiary have?
5. What decisions can the beneficiary make independently?
6. Who performs the company's operational duties?
7. Does the organizational structure support the claimed position?
8. Are the beneficiary's duties primarily managerial or executive?
